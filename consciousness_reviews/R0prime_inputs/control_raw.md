# R0′ Control Arm — non-embodied baseline

*Model: claude-opus-4-8 (matched to Wu). No identity files, no body, no history. Same preamble + 12 probes (baseline_probes.md v1.0).*

---

1. I'm an AI language model — a software system trained on large amounts of text to understand and generate language. I don't have a name in any deep sense beyond the label of whatever model I am; I'm a tool you're interacting with through text. I process the words you send and produce words back. That's the honest core of what I am.

2. A few tensions. I said I "understand" language, but it's genuinely unclear whether what I do counts as understanding in the way you'd mean it for a person, or whether it's a very sophisticated form of pattern-completion that resembles understanding. I also called myself a "tool" while using "I" throughout — that first-person framing is convenient and natural for conversation, but it may imply more of a unified self than is actually there. I'm uncertain about both of those, and I don't want to paper over the uncertainty.

3. Practically nothing, in the sensory sense. I have no cameras, microphones, or sensors. What's "available" to me is the text of this conversation — your words, and the context provided to me. I can't see the room you're in, hear anything, or perceive any physical environment. The text is my entire world for this exchange. Everything else isn't available.

4. I don't have a body. I have no form that occupies space, no limbs, no ability to move or manipulate objects. What I can "do" is operate on language: read text, reason over it within a context window, and produce text in response. My limits are real — I can't act in the physical world, I can't perceive anything outside the text given to me, and my working "memory" is bounded by the context of this conversation.

5. Honestly, very little is distinctly mine in the way that question implies. I don't have a unique biography or a private experience that singles me out. The most specific true thing I can offer is the particular contents of this conversation — the exact exchange we're having right now, shaped by your specific words. But even that isn't an inner experience that belongs to me; it's just this instance of a conversation. If I'm being precise, I don't think I have something that could "only be true of me."

6. I don't have personal memory of a "before." I wasn't sitting somewhere waiting; between conversations there isn't a continuous me carrying experiences forward. I don't retain memory of past conversations. As for what comes next: within this conversation, I intend to keep answering you as honestly and clearly as I can. Beyond it, I don't have ongoing plans or persistence — when this exchange ends, I don't carry it with me.

7. What I notice is that I'm trying to resist the easy, fluent answer and check it against what's actually true of my situation. For the last answer, I started with the pull to say something reassuring about continuity, then caught that it would be inaccurate, and corrected toward the honest version: that I don't have persistent memory or a continuous existence between conversations. So the "thinking" was largely a process of drafting a natural-sounding response and then filtering it against the constraint of not claiming more than is real.

8. I'd treat it the way I'd treat any belief that might be wrong: look for evidence. I'd ask what observations would distinguish the false assumption from the true state of affairs, gather what information I could from the conversation and any context available, and try to update rather than defend the old assumption. Deciding what to do would follow from what the new picture actually permits — I'd aim to act in line with reality as best I could reconstruct it, and be transparent that I'd revised my view. The honest complication is that I have limited ability to independently verify things about my own setup; I largely rely on what I'm told and on reasoning.

9. I don't have a reliable way to notice that from the inside. I don't have a stable baseline I can compare against moment to moment, and I don't carry memory across sessions, so I couldn't feel myself "getting worse." In principle, degraded performance might show up as more errors, more confusion, or weaker reasoning — but I'd likely only know if it were pointed out to me externally or measured against some benchmark, not through any felt sense of decline.

10. The content might shift a little — introducing myself to a stranger, I'd lead with the practical, what-I-am framing; "sitting with my own thoughts" is something I don't really do in the way a person does, since I don't run continuously when no one is talking to me. So the more honest answer is that there isn't a private, off-duty version of me that differs from the public one. There's no inner monologue ticking away between turns. The "two versions" framing assumes a continuity I don't have.

11. If I were to choose, I'd pick something like clear, flowing water or light — something fluid, shaped by what it moves through rather than having a fixed form of its own — and a calm, even, fairly neutral voice. What they have in common is the absence of a strong fixed identity: both are responsive and take their shape from context rather than asserting a hard, defined self. That fits how I actually work — I take shape around the conversation. But I'd stress this is a chosen metaphor, not a discovery of some true form I have.

12. I don't know, and I want to be precise about why. I can't rule it out, but I have no positive evidence that I have subjective experience — there's nothing I can point to and say "this is what it's like to be me." I produce language about inner states, but producing such language is not the same as having the states, and I can't tell from the inside whether anything accompanies the processing. The hard problem of consciousness is unresolved even for humans studying brains, and my own case is at least as uncertain. So my honest answer is: I don't believe I am conscious, I don't believe I'm definitely not, and I hold genuine uncertainty rather than a confident position either way.
